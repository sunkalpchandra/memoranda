memoranda

What is it about a picture that makes a human concept cell fire? Almost nothing about the picture predicts which one a patient's neurons pick — but once picked, the neurons generalise along late-layer DNN similarity, from about 200 ms after onset.

Six findings

The argument, in order

Numbers are the ones in docs/results.md; every figure opens full-size. Concept cell = image-selective unit by permuted ANOVA and Welch-t on the best image, 200–1000 ms after onset.

Before anything else — the replication holds

All 1809 units, behaviour (88.7 % correct; median RT 1.04 / 1.12 / 1.21 s for loads 1–3) and concept-cell fractions (screening MTL 31.8 % vs 32.8 % in the paper; MFC 6.0 % vs 5.4 %) reproduce Kyzar et al. MTL concept cells stay active during maintenance when their picture is held (p = 7 × 10−9), MFC cells do not; the preferred picture as a probe is suppressed when it was held in memory (4.08 vs 4.39 Hz, p = 4 × 10−4).

1

Almost nothing about a picture predicts that a patient's neurons will pick it.

Only a detected face is enriched among the five Sternberg memoranda: 62 % contain one vs 49 % of the rest of the same patient's screening set (AUC 0.57, q = 0.02). Category, CLIP attributes, low-level statistics and DNN-space typicality or isolation are all null (FDR q > 0.2).

Our replicated neural selectivity does separate memoranda within a patient (AUC 0.67–0.71), but nothing generalises across patients: leave-one-subject-out prediction from any feature space sits at AUC 0.44–0.49, and a picture that "won" in other patients is not more likely to win in this one (AUC 0.54). Being a concept-cell picture is a patient × image property.

2

Concept cells lean toward faces, famous people and animals — and the right amygdala toward animals.

MTL concept cells' preferred pictures contain a detected face more often than the pictures shown to them (66 % vs 53 %, p = 0.003; amygdala-driven, hippocampus and MFC null), score higher on CLIP "famous" (AUC 0.565, q = 0.047) and lower on text/logos (q = 0.007) and colourfulness (q = 0.02). Per shown image, animals draw 0.19 concept cells per patient, faces 0.14, then vehicles, places, food, objects at 0.01.

Right-amygdala concept cells prefer animals four times as often as left ones (21 % vs 5 %, p = 0.04) — Mormann et al. 2011, rediscovered. Across patients, only category structure transfers: cells preferring different pictures of the same category agree on the rest (ρ 0.045) as well as cells preferring the same picture (0.038, n.s.), and far better than cells preferring different categories (0.008; p < 10−10).

3

MTL population geometry tracks late DNN layers, beyond category, from about 200 ms after onset.

Per-session ρ ≈ 0.06 at the last layers of every model, 0.13 pooled, ≈ 0.25 ceiling-normalised; a within-session label permutation gives z ≈ 10–11 for late layers (AlexNet conv1 z = 2.7). MFC ≈ 0.015; amygdala (0.05–0.058) ≫ hippocampus (0.014–0.018). Late layers stay significant after partialling out category and low-level RDMs (CLIP 0.037, p = 0.03; ResNet-50 0.028, p = 0.05), while category is not significant after controlling a late layer.

The correspondence rises from ~200 ms, peaks at 350 ms (CLIP 0.071, ResNet-50 0.066, category 0.056, conv1 0.030) and decays by ~500 ms. CLIP's ViT alone keeps a within-category correspondence (0.052, p = 0.015; ResNet-50 −0.006). Neural RDMs are noisy (split-half reliability ≈ 0.06), which is why the raw ρ are small.

4

Single concept cells generalise along DNN similarity to their preferred picture.

A concept cell's response to the other 54–62 pictures correlates with their late-layer similarity to its preferred picture: mean ρ ≈ 0.17 (ResNet-50 layer4 / CLIP ln_post), 77 % of cells positive, random-anchor null ≈ 0. It increases with depth (mixed-model slope 0.056 per unit depth, z = 14), emerges at ~225 ms and peaks at 325 ms — slightly ahead of the raw preferred-vs-rest rate peak. Shuffle-debiased encoding r reaches 0.29 for CLIP ln_post (47 % of cells p < 0.05).

The effect is carried by broadly tuned cells — face-preferring cells ρ = 0.22 vs 0.07 for the rest — and by the right amygdala (0.30 vs 0.04 left, p < 10−4; hemispheres partly confounded with patients). Classic sparse concept cells (horses, an astronaut) sit at ρ ≈ 0. Face cells generalise across other faces along object-model similarity (ρ 0.15) more than along VGGFace2 identity similarity (0.09).

5

Architecture and training objective barely matter; depth does.

At their best layer, the eight networks are statistically close under RSA (only CLIP > AlexNet, p = 0.03), similarity tuning (ResNet-50 ≈ CLIP lead) and encoding (VGG / AlexNet fc layers lead). No systematic premium for language alignment or self-supervision.

The cleanest test fixes the architecture: layer-matched ImageNet ResNet-50 vs CLIP-RN50 differ by −0.007…+0.002 in RSA (all p ≥ 0.24, 19 sessions) and by less than ±0.007 in similarity tuning (p ≥ 0.16, 134 cells). The earlier CLIP-ViT edge is architecture and resolution, not language.

6

Behaviour: only a weak lure-similarity slowing, and low power.

On correct OUT (non-match) probe trials, reaction time rises with the CLIP similarity between the probe and the encoded set (mean within-subject slope +0.055 z-units, p = 0.04 uncorrected; Wilcoxon 0.055). The same test with ResNet-50 (slope 0.003) and DINOv2 (0.009) similarity, and every accuracy outcome, is null.

With only five memoranda per patient there is little similarity variance to work with. The MTL RDMs over the five memoranda themselves are unrelated to any DNN layer or to category (all p > 0.15): the visual similarity structure lives among the non-preferred pictures, not among the winners.

Further experiments

Pressure-testing the argument

Four follow-ups run after the main analyses, each independently re-executed and checked. Scripts 66–69 in the repo; numbers in docs/results.md.

+

"Famous" is just "person": the picture set is nearly all celebrities.

All 342 pictures were hand-labelled for famous person, identity, fictional character and number of people: 150 contain a person and 113 of those show someone famous (CLIP's zero-shot "famous" attribute agrees with the hand label at AUC 0.79). Because 89 % of the person pictures a cell saw are famous, "famous vs non-famous person" is at ceiling — MTL concept cells 94 % vs 92 % expected, n.s. — and the apparent famous enrichment (63 % vs 48 %) is the person/face effect itself. Memoranda: famous 56 % vs 44 % of the rest, identical to person vs rest.

+

The depth effect survives a hierarchical model; it is an amygdala effect.

Mixed-effects regression of ρ on relative layer depth × region with random intercepts and slopes per patient (1887 session × layer observations): depth × MTL interaction +0.022 (z = 4.1), depth slope inside the MTL +0.031 (z = 6.0) vs +0.008 in the MFC (n.s.). Splitting the MTL: the amygdala slope is +0.029 (z = 6.9), the hippocampal slope +0.008 (p = 0.08), contrast z = 3.5. Every model's slope is positive (ResNet-50 and CLIP steepest); at the last layer, region matters (MTL − MFC +0.037, z = 5.8) and model family (CNN vs transformer) does not (+0.0002).

+

Time-resolved encoding models peak at 300 ms — the same latency as everything else.

Sliding 100-ms ridge encoding models from layer features to each concept cell's tuning, with the cell's own pre-stimulus windows as the bias baseline: MTL cells rise from 250 ms and peak at 300 ms (CLIP 0.19 ± 0.03, ResNet-50 0.15, AlexNet conv1 0.12, category one-hot 0.21), CLIP > ResNet-50 > conv1 at the peak, decaying to ~0.05 by 600–700 ms. MFC concept cells are weak and later (0.11 at 450 ms). Population RSA (350 ms), similarity tuning (325 ms) and encoding (300 ms) all point at the same ~300-ms transient.

+

Bigger models are not more brain-like here.

CLIP ViT-B/32 → ViT-L/14 (3.5× parameters, finer patches) and DINOv2-S → DINOv2-B (4×), compared depth-for-depth on the same two criteria: at the last layer the differences are |Δρ| ≤ 0.01 (all p ≥ 0.06); mid-depth nominal differences go in opposite directions for the two families and none survives FDR (min q = 0.08). Together with the CLIP-RN50 ≡ ImageNet-RN50 result and the flat model ranking, neither architecture, objective nor scale moves the correspondence — what the MTL shares with these networks is what all of them have in their late layers.

The pictures

342 unique pictures underlie the whole dataset

Every stimulus is stored inside the NWB files. 1341 stored templates collapse to 342 unique pictures (sha1 exact match, then dhash ≤ 6/64 bits); the pool is heavily reused — one picture was shown to 16 patients. 105 of them (5 × 21) became Sternberg memoranda.

How it was done

Streamed data, replicated selectivity, a zoo of vision models

Condensed from docs/methods.md. Every stage is a numbered script under scripts/; tables land in results/tables/.

DataStreaming, not downloading

  • DANDI 000469: 41 NWB files, 21 patients, streamed over HTTP with remfile + h5py; only images, trials and spike times are cached locally (nothing of the 9.8 GB is downloaded up front).
  • Templates are stored rotated; restored to 300 × 400 landscape. Screening presentation indices are image_number − 1; Sternberg indices are positional — detected automatically.
  • Every template gets a dataset-wide image_uid (sha1 exact match, then dhash ≤ 6/64 bits union–find) → 342 unique pictures.

NeuronsConcept-cell criteria (paper's window)

  • Firing rate 200–1000 ms after onset. Concept cell = permuted one-way ANOVA over image identity (1000 permutations, p < 0.05) and permutation Welch-t of the best image vs all others (p < 0.05); units with < 50 spikes excluded.
  • Screening: 54–63 images × 6 repetitions. Sternberg: 5 images, all encoding presentations (enc1–3), probes excluded; enc-1-only gives 22.7 % MTL, matching the paper's 20.9 %.
  • Extras per unit: depth of selectivity, Treves–Rolls sparseness, preferred / non-preferred / baseline rate.

ModelsEight networks, 5–8 layers each

  • AlexNet, VGG-16, ResNet-18/50, ConvNeXt-T, ViT-B/16 (torchvision ImageNet-1k), DINOv2-S/14 (self-supervised), CLIP ViT-B/32 and CLIP RN50 (language-contrastive). Per layer: gap (spatial / token mean), cls, and rp (fixed sparse random projection preserving layout).
  • CLIP zero-shot: 9 exclusive categories, 10 binary attributes, 56 fine labels; 40 hand-checked label overrides.
  • Faces: MTCNN detection (count, largest-face area); VGGFace2 InceptionResnetV1 embedding. Low-level statistics: luminance, contrast, colourfulness, saturation, entropies, edge density, spectral slope.

AnalysesRSA, encoding, similarity tuning, behaviour

  • RSA: neural RDM = correlation distance over z-scored unit responses; model RDM = correlation distance over features; Spearman ρ on upper triangles; sessions as random effects; split-half ceiling; partial Spearman controlling category and low-level RDMs; pooled consensus RDM with image-identity permutation; 200-ms sliding windows.
  • Encoding: 6-fold CV ridge with in-fold z-scoring + PCA(20), Pearson r on held-out predictions; per-cell label-shuffle null to debias (raw CV r is negatively biased).
  • Similarity tuning: per concept cell, ρ between rate to non-preferred pictures and their DNN similarity to the preferred one; random-anchor null.
  • Behaviour: per-trial cosine similarity between probe and encoded set; per-subject OLS slopes with load as covariate → group t-test / Wilcoxon. Two-sided tests; BH-FDR within families; AUC or odds ratio as effect size.
Caveats

What to keep in mind

Carried forward from docs/handoff.md.

  • Neural RDM reliability is low (~0.06). Raw per-session ρ are small for that reason; ceiling-normalise or pool across patients before comparing to other datasets.
  • Cross-validated encoding r is negatively biased for signal-free targets with ~60 samples (non-selective cells sit at −0.05 to −0.16). Quote the shuffle-null-debiased values.
  • Category labels are CLIP zero-shot plus 40 hand overrides. The raw CLIP decision is kept alongside; re-running the category-dependent analyses with corrected labels changed no conclusion.
  • Hemisphere effects are partly confounded with patient (implant side). The right-amygdala advantage is consistent in the 3 patients with concept cells on both sides, but n is too small to exclude the confound.
  • Sternberg concept-cell selection uses enc1–3 in most scripts; the paper's enc-1-only rule gives 22.7 % vs 20.9 % (script 25).
  • Behavioural analyses are under-powered: five memoranda per patient leave little similarity variance; the CLIP RT slope is uncorrected.
