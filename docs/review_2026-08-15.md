# Adversarial statistical / logic review — 2026-08-15

Scope: `docs/results.md`, `docs/methods.md`, `docs/decisions.md`, `README.md`, and the scripts
`20`, `30`, `40`, `47`, `51`, `58`, `62`, `memoranda/neural.py`, `analysis/rsa.py`,
`analysis/tables.py` (+ `analysis/encoding.py`, `analysis/stats.py` where needed to follow the
logic). All numbers below were re-derived from `results/tables/*.csv` and
`data/manifests/*.csv` in this session; nothing was re-run.

Bottom line: the six headline findings survive, but three of them are stated more strongly than
the tables support, several p-values are computed at the wrong unit of inference (cells or
subject×image pairs pooled across patients), and a handful of numbers in `docs/results.md` are
stale (pre-label-correction) or refer to a different layer than the sentence claims.

---

## Issues (ordered by severity)

### HIGH

**H1. Headline 2 ("only a detected face is enriched among memoranda") is the one significant
result out of 52 contrasts, and it is significant only because FDR was run inside a 3-test
"faces" family that was added after the other 49 tests came back null.**
`scripts/30_stimset_analysis.py:105` (`fdr_bh` per block), `:172` (faces block added
later, cf. "A2 amendment" in `docs/results.md:153`); `docs/results.md:6-8, 153-157`;
`README.md:63-65`.
Family-wide BH over all 52 A2 contrasts gives q = 0.29 for `largest_face_area` and
`face_found` (block-wise q = 0.02). The pooled Mann–Whitney p (0.012/0.013) also treats
~1200 subject×image pairs as independent although pictures are shared across patients (one
picture is shown to 16 subjects) — the within-subject AUC (0.569 ± 0.025, n = 20 subjects)
does support the direction (t ≈ 2.8, p ≈ 0.01 vs 0.5), which is the test that should be
reported. Additionally, memoranda were *chosen by the original authors from neural
selectivity*, and A5 shows MTL concept cells prefer faces, so this is a downstream
consequence of A5, not an independent picture-level finding.
*Fix:* (a) report family-wide q (0.29) alongside the block q, or pre-register the faces
block as a confirmatory test of A5 and say so; (b) use the within-subject AUC t-test (or a
mixed logistic model with subject random intercept) as the inferential test; (c) rewrite
headline 2 as "no picture-level property except (weakly) a detected face — itself expected
from the concept cells' face bias — distinguishes memoranda".

**H2. Cell-level and pair-level pooling across patients is used for most single-neuron
p-values; several headline p-values are therefore over-precise.**
`scripts/47_similarity_tuning.py:74-78` (Wilcoxon over 134 cells from 16 patients);
`scripts/62_hemisphere.py:61` (Mann–Whitney over 63 vs 38 cells);
`scripts/31` → `A5_face_preference.csv` (MWU over 134 cells, p = 0.003);
`scripts/36` → `A5_cross_patient_tuning.csv` (1672 vs 2985 *pairs*, p < 1e-10);
`scripts/55` face-similarity (89 cells); `scripts/51` (sem over 134 cells);
`docs/methods.md:63` says "sessions/subjects are the unit of inference for group tests" —
this is not what the scripts do.
Two patients (13, 14) contribute 56/134 MTL concept cells (42 %) and have the largest
effects (similarity ρ 0.32 / 0.23; face-pref excess +0.24 / +0.28). Re-derived checks:
* Similarity tuning (CLIP ln_post): cell-pooled mean ρ 0.170, Wilcoxon p = 2e-12; **subject-
  mean ρ 0.100**, Wilcoxon over 16 subjects p = 0.016; cluster-robust (by subject) p = 2e-4.
  Survives, but the honest effect size is ~0.10–0.17, not 0.17.
* Face preference of MTL concept cells (66 % vs 53 %): unweighted subject-level Wilcoxon
  p = 0.67 (10/16 positive); cluster-robust p = 0.004; amygdala cluster-robust p = 7e-4.
  Survives with clustering, but the naive p = 0.003 and "amygdala-driven" need the caveat.
* Right vs left amygdala similarity tuning: subject-level MWU on per-subject means (9 R vs
  8 L patients) p = 0.008 — survives, and is the number to quote instead of "p < 1e-4".
* Face-preferring cells: object-model vs VGGFace2 similarity — the docs' "all p < 1e-4"
  are *vs zero*; the paired difference (the actual claim) is p = 0.026 (ResNet-50) /
  0.009 (CLIP) at cell level and **p = 0.34 at subject level** (n = 13). Not established.
* Cross-patient "same category > different category, p < 1e-10": each cell enters
  hundreds of pairs; the p is meaningless as stated (see M6).
*Fix:* report cluster-robust or mixed-model p-values (script 59 already does this for two
contrasts — extend it to face preference, laterality, face-space and cross-patient tests),
and quote subject-level means next to cell-pooled means. Rewrite the methods sentence.

**H3. The MFC "concept cell" population is statistically indistinguishable from the ANOVA
false-positive rate, and the post-hoc "max vs rest" test is a non-test as implemented.**
`memoranda/neural.py:134-156` (`permuted_max_vs_rest`), `:251`.
The permutation null for the post-hoc test compares t(max image vs rest) against t of a
*random* image-sized subset vs rest, not against the *maximum* t under permutation. It
therefore passes 630/907 screening units (69 %) and adds nothing to the ANOVA (163 units
pass ANOVA+post-hoc, 168 pass ANOVA alone). The concept-cell criterion is thus ANOVA p < 0.05
alone → expected false-positive rate 5 %. MFC: 29/486 = 6.0 % (95 % binomial band under
5 %: 3.2–7.2 %). So the n = 29 MFC concept cells used in A4/A8 ("MFC concept − MTL
non-concept +0.092, z = 3.1", "MFC concept cells CLIP 0.21 (38 %)", "dACC concept cells
strongly late-tuned") may be largely false positives; ~21 of the 134 MTL concept cells are
also expected false positives (attenuates, does not inflate, MTL effects). This replicates
the paper's numbers, so it is fine as a *replication*, but not as a basis for MFC claims.
*Fix:* (a) in `permuted_max_vs_rest`, compare `t_obs` with the max-over-images t of each
permutation (or drop the test and say the criterion is ANOVA-only); (b) add a sentence in
A0 that MFC concept-cell prevalence equals the nominal false-positive rate, and either drop
MFC-concept-cell subgroup claims or label them "not distinguishable from chance selection".

### MEDIUM

**M1. "MTL − MFC difference z ≈ 6" (script 58) is not a test of MTL ≠ MFC.**
`scripts/58_group_permutation.py:90-93`; `docs/results.md:175, 200-202`.
The null shuffles picture identity in *both* regions, so H0 is "neither region has any
structure"; the difference statistic then just re-tests MTL ≠ 0 with a small MFC penalty
(obs 0.044 / null sd 0.007). The proper contrasts are the paired tests: paired t over 17
sessions gives p = 0.145 for CLIP ln_post (cls) and p = 0.096 for ResNet-50 avgpool
(`A3_rsa_mtl_vs_mfc.csv`); the mixed model gives p = 0.064. Docs line 56 says "marginal
(p ≈ 0.07) for the rest" — most late layers are p = 0.08–0.2. Headline 3 "MFC flat" and
README "amygdala ≫ hippocampus / MTL ≫ MFC" therefore rest on fixed-effects tests only.
*Fix:* relabel the script-58 row as "difference vs a both-null baseline"; state MTL > MFC as
"decisive as a fixed effect, marginal (p 0.06–0.15) across patients". Note that MFC itself
is significantly > 0 under the same fixed-effects null (z ≈ 3, p ≤ 0.007) — "MFC flat"
should read "MFC weak and without a depth gradient".

**M2. "Debiased r" (obs − null mean) is not a bias-free metric; the CLIP-is-best and
category-on-par claims are artefacts of predictor-specific null means.**
`scripts/51_encoding_null.py:77, 81`; `docs/results.md:186-194, 232-236`; headline 4.
Null means range from −0.09 (VGG fc7) to −0.21 (category one-hot, CLIP). Raw r: VGG fc7
0.101 > AlexNet fc6 0.097 > CLIP 0.085 > … > category −0.007. Subtracting a predictor-
specific offset re-orders the models purely by how negative their null is (CLIP 0.285,
category 0.206 ≈ AlexNet fc6 0.205). Under H1 the fold-mean anticorrelation that produces
the negative bias is diluted, so the offset is not additive. `frac_sig` (empirical per-cell
p) is the defensible comparison: CLIP 47 % > DINOv2 40 % > AlexNet 39 % > ResNet 34 % ≈
VGG 34 % > category 32 % — CLIP still leads, but "category on par with CNN fc layers" is
not supported by that column.
*Fix:* report per-cell z = (obs − null mean)/null sd or `frac_sig` as the primary metric;
or remove the bias at source (evaluate r on fold-centred predictions / use CV R² with a
fixed penalty grid) so no subtraction is needed. Reword headline 4.

**M3. Headline 3 / README: "rising monotonically with depth in every architecture, peaking
at the last layers" — not true for four of eight models.**
`docs/results.md:9, 51-52`; `README.md:81`.
Per-session MTL means: AlexNet peaks at conv4 (0.052) and plateaus (fc7 0.048); VGG conv4_3
0.050 ≈ logits 0.054; DINOv2-S peaks at block8 (0.043) and falls to 0.033 at `norm`
(script 67 finds its depth slope n.s.); ConvNeXt stage4 0.041 < logits 0.050. Pooled RDM:
AlexNet conv4 0.102 > fc7 0.085. The correct statement is "rises from early to mid/late
layers and plateaus".
*Fix:* reword; keep "monotonic" only for ResNet-50 / CLIP where it holds.

**M4. Internal contradiction: "CLIP alone keeps within-category correspondence" (headline
3, README:91, `docs/results.md:80-84`) vs. "training objective makes no measurable
difference" (headline 5, script 63) — and the within-category table itself.**
`A3_within_category_rsa.csv`: within-category pairs (all cats): CLIP ln_post 0.052
(p = 0.015) **and ViT-B/16 ln 0.030 (p = 0.016)** — an ImageNet-supervised model. So the
sentence "language-aligned features carry within-category structure that ImageNet features
lack" is contradicted by the same table; script 63 shows CLIP-RN50 ≡ RN50. The remaining
"within faces only: CLIP 0.042, ResNet-50 0.038 (p ≈ 0.1–0.14)" numbers are stale — the
current table has 0.024 (p = 0.38) and 0.023 (p = 0.22).
*Fix:* rewrite as "ViT-family late layers (CLIP and ImageNet ViT) keep a within-category
correspondence that ResNet/DINOv2 do not (n = 19 sessions, uncorrected over 7 layers)"; drop
"CLIP alone" from headline 3; update the within-faces numbers.

**M5. "Late layers add to and largely subsume the category RDM" is over-read from two
marginal tests.**
`docs/results.md:11-12, 60-63, 141-144`. Partial ρ | category+low-level: CLIP 0.037
p = 0.031, ResNet-50 0.029 p = 0.041 (`A3_partial_rsa.csv`); category | CLIP p = 0.08 (n.s.).
"Subsumes" is an absence-of-evidence claim at p = 0.08 with n = 19; the category RDM alone
scores 0.055 per session and 0.117 pooled vs 0.060 / 0.129 for the best DNN layer, and the
commonality analysis (`A3_variance_partition.csv`) now puts the *shared-by-all-three*
component (0.57 %) above unique-CLIP (0.39 %). The honest reading is "late layers and the
category RDM are largely redundant; late layers carry a small extra component (p ≈ 0.03–0.04)".
Note also the commonality numbers in `docs/results.md:106-108` are stale (unique CLIP 0.44 →
0.39; shared all three 0.44 → 0.57; shared CLIP+ResNet 0.31 → 0.18; unique ResNet 0.24 → 0.20).
*Fix:* reword and update the numbers.

**M6. Cross-patient tuning: pair-level p < 1e-10 and an under-powered null.**
`docs/results.md:131-136`; `README.md:74-76`. 1672 vs 2985 pairs built from ~130 cells —
pairs sharing a cell are dependent, so the p is not interpretable. And "same preferred picture

*Fix:* permute cell-to-patient labels or use a mixed model with crossed cell random effects;
state the same-picture result as "underpowered (n = 43 pairs, 95 % CI ≈ −0.02…0.10)".

### LOW

**L1. Sessions with 2–4 MTL units enter the RSA group test.** `scripts/40:76` (`< 2`);
sub-17 has 2 units (correlation distance over 2 z-scored units is degenerate), sub-12 has 4.
The result is robust (≥ 5 units: n = 16, mean 0.066) and to dropping the two most influential
sessions 13/14 (mean halves to 0.030 but t-test p improves to 0.004; depth gradient intact),
so this is cosmetic — but the mean ρ ≈ 0.06 is inflated by two sessions (median 0.034);
quote the median or the leave-two-out mean too. n_units correlates with ρ (Spearman 0.50,
p = 0.036) — say so when comparing amygdala (median 11 units) with hippocampus (8).

**L2. Random-anchor null in script 47 is a single draw per cell and is not exactly matched.**
`scripts/47:62-66`. Observed: preferred image excluded; null: anchor j excluded but the
*preferred* image (the extreme rate) is kept. Null means are not ≈ 0 for all layers
(AlexNet conv1 0.049, ResNet-50 layer3 0.033, CLIP embed 0.045; conv1's p_vs_null_anchor is
0.23 while p_vs_zero ≈ 0). "null ≈ 0" is true only for the headline layers. Fix: exclude
both pref and j in the null, average ≥ 20 random anchors, and say which layers the null
statement covers.

**L3. Multiple comparisons across the layer sweeps are uncorrected** (≈ 60 layers × 6
regions in script 40; 52 layers × 3 groups in script 47; 28 model pairs in script 49; 70
MTL−MFC rows in `A3_rsa_mtl_vs_mfc.csv`). Headline layers were chosen after seeing the
sweep. The depth-trend and permutation-z results are strong enough that this does not
change conclusions, but "only CLIP > AlexNet (p = 0.03)" out of 28 pairs should be
described as chance-level, which actually strengthens headline 5.

**L4. A2 "memoranda are separated by our replicated neural selectivity (AUC 0.67–0.71)"
is circular by construction** — the memoranda were chosen by the original authors from these
same recordings. `docs/results.md:41`; README:66-67 lists it beside picture-level predictors.
Label it a sanity check.

**L5. Mixed-model z's in A8 treat layers within a cell/session as independent** (6968 obs
from 134 cells; docs note this only for script 67). Add the caveat to script 59's line.

**L6. A5 laterality claims are cell-level Fisher / MWU on cells pooled across patients**
(animal preference 13/63 vs 2/38, p = 0.044) and are one of many area × hemisphere ×
category tests; the a-priori Mormann et al. hypothesis justifies the test but the docs
should say "one pre-specified test, cell-level, uncorrected". Subject-level version of the
similarity-tuning laterality (p = 0.008, 9 vs 8 patients) is fine and should be quoted.

**L7. VGGFace2 vs object-model comparison is not input-matched**: the VGGFace2 embedding is
of the largest MTCNN crop (whole picture if none), object models see the full picture. A
lower ρ for VGGFace2 can reflect crop/quality, not "identity vs object features".

## Three things done well

1. **The negative CV-r bias was caught and quantified** (D6, script 51) instead of being
   reported as "encoding r ≈ 0.10"; the label-shuffle null per cell is the right instrument
   even if the subtraction is not the right summary (M2).
2. **Two complementary nulls for the RSA result** — across-session t on Fisher-z (random
   effects) and within-session picture-identity permutation (fixed effects, script 58) — with
   the docs explicitly stating which question each answers (:176-178). Split-half ceilings,
   window robustness (script 65), rp-view check (56), label-correction re-runs (16) and the
   hand-labelled famous set (66) are exactly the robustness checks a sceptic would ask for.
3. **Nulls are reported as nulls**: LOSO preference prediction (0.44–0.49), Sternberg-5
   geometry, model scale, objective contrast (63), famous-beyond-person, and the "sparse
   concept cells have ρ ≈ 0" exemplars are all written up rather than buried, and the
   similarity-tuning analysis correctly excludes the preferred image from the correlation.

## Suggested edits to the six headline findings

1. unchanged, but add: "concept-cell criterion is effectively ANOVA-only; MFC prevalence
   (6.0 %) equals the nominal false-positive rate".
2. "No picture-level property predicts a patient's memoranda (52 contrasts, all family-wide
   q ≥ 0.29); a detected face is the only nominal hit (62 % vs 49 %, within-subject AUC 0.57)
   and is expected from the concept cells' face bias."
3. "…rising from early to late layers in every architecture (plateauing mid-late in AlexNet /
   VGG / DINOv2)… MTL > MFC decisive as fixed effect, marginal across patients (p 0.06–0.15);
   late layers and the category RDM are largely redundant, late layers add a small unique
   component (p ≈ 0.03–0.04); ViT-family (CLIP and ImageNet ViT) late layers keep a
   within-category correspondence." Drop "CLIP alone".
4. "…ρ ≈ 0.17 cell-pooled / 0.10 subject-mean, 71–77 % positive, cluster-robust p < 1e-3;
   frac. of cells with significant CLIP encoding 47 %…; right > left amygdala (subject-level
   p = 0.008; hemisphere partly confounded with patient)."
5. unchanged (the uncorrected 1/28 pairwise hit supports it).
6. "No reliable behavioural effect (best of 30 uncorrected tests p = 0.04)."
